Claim 1 follows from the **unrestricted DE path**, not merely from arithmetic on the quoted switch. The [header’s mask](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/include/thirdparty/crmfbreg.h:328) admits sixteen encodings; [`horrible_getputpixel()`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_sgi_re.c:284) handles four explicitly. The other twelve—`0x40` through `0xf0` in increments of `0x10`—select `default:`. Nothing in the DE writer or `draw_primitive()` restricts these to the five named types.

There **are earlier returns** in [`horrible_getputpixel()`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_sgi_re.c:196): coordinate bounds, tile-index bounds, unsupported addressing modes and the tile-pointer validity check. These restrict whether a pixel operation proceeds; they do not validate the color-type field. The twelve-count applies when those addressing checks pass.

One denominator correction: a YCRCB-only check covers **1/12 of the default-arm encodings**, and **1/16 of the entire color-type field**.

For claim 2, **eight-byte depth is reachable, and CI is not restricted to one-byte depth**. The DE writer preserves bits 9:8, and the helper evaluates `1 << ((mode >> 8) & 3)` without rejecting the fourth encoding. The [header names only three buffer-depth encodings](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/include/thirdparty/crmfbreg.h:325); that naming imposes no runtime restriction.

The MTE path independently confirms that these mismatches survive actual argument construction. The callee **does copy the requested length on an ordinary RAM write**. [`mips_cpu_new()`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_mips.c:110) installs `mips_memory_rw`; [the generated MIPS inclusion](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/tmp_mips_tail.c:38) instantiates `cpus/memory_rw.c`.

In that implementation:

- [`mips_memory_rw()` and its worker](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/memory_rw.c:183) bypass virtual translation for `PHYSICAL` and retain `len`.
- The worker’s RAM write is literally [`memcpy(memblock + offset, data, len)`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/memory_rw.c:779). Crossing a memory-block boundary splits the transfer and forwards the remaining bytes; it does not truncate the total.
- There **is device-boundary clamping**, and `NO_EXCEPTIONS` can suppress device-handler calls. Those are [MMIO-dispatch behaviors](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/memory_rw.c:480), not padding or sanitization of a RAM-write source buffer. The nearby zero-fill operations apply to reads.

Therefore **the DE line and rectangle loops can produce a guest-driven per-pixel output flood**. The GET color-default arm also prints on every invocation. The earlier `SGIRE_W_BADMODE` latch guards the addressing-mode diagnostic only. Although MTE also calls the helper per pixel, its current CI/RGBA construction does **not** reach the color-default diagnostic.

I found an additional issue in the GET half: **it can consume more bytes than it reads**. [`horrible_getputpixel()` GET](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_sgi_re.c:319) requests `bufdepth` bytes, but RGB consumes `buf[0..2]`, while RGBA, ABGR and default consume `buf[0..3]`. At depths one or two, those arms use uninitialised trailing bytes. CI GET has no such mismatch. The source GET result feeds the later PUT in `draw_primitive()`’s rectangle loop, so this can also propagate into guest memory.

The current RGB GET expression does incorporate the `#505` layout correction. That correction leaves the GET length mismatch intact.
