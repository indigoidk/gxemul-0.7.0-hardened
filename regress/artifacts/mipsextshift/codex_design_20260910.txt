The defect is confirmed, with important corrections: **plain DEXT has no bad field pairs; DEXTM and DEXTU supply the bad pairs to `X(dext)`; the DINS-family execution handlers do not exist in this tree.**

The [decoder](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_mips_instr.c:4359) independently extracts five-bit `rd` and `sa`. The [SPECIAL3 cases](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_mips_instr.c:5577) perform no field-sum validation. DEXTM adds 32 to `msbd`; DEXTU adds 32 to `lsb`; both adjustments happen before packing the arguments for the shared handler.

I exhaustively enumerated all 1,024 raw field pairs per opcode, including packing and unpacking:

| Opcode | Handler `msbd` | Handler `lsb` | `size` | Middle count, before cast | Negative pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EXT | 0–31 | 0–31 | 1–32 | −31–31 | 496 |
| DEXT | 0–31 | 0–31 | 1–32 | 1–63 | 0 |
| DEXTM | 32–63 | 0–31 | 33–64 | −31–31 | 496 |
| DEXTU | 0–31 | 32–63 | 1–32 | −31–31 | 496 |

For DEXTM/U, the negative counts become `2^64−31` through `2^64−1` after conversion to `uint64_t`. The cast does not repair them.

There **are admission gates**, contrary to the broader claim in the filed row: SPECIAL3 rejects older ISA/revision combinations, and R5900 takes another path. Also, `rt == $zero` selects NOP. Thus the field domains are unconstrained **when the extract handler actually runs**, but not every MIPS model or destination reaches it.

I found and read the manuals under the parent project’s `_scratchpad`. [MIPS32 Revision 6.04, page 173](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/_scratchpad/mips32_volII.txt:647) calls EXT’s excessive field sum UNPREDICTABLE. [MIPS64 Revision 2.50, pages 133–137](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/_scratchpad/mips64_volII.txt:554) explicitly says ordinary DEXT has no UNPREDICTABLE field case, and specifies UNPREDICTABLE for excessive normalized sums in DEXTM/U. The [published MIPS64 manual](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/downloads-mips/documents/MD00087-2B-MIPS64BIS-AFP-05.04.pdf) corroborates those bounds.

**RI is permitted here, not mandated.** The local manual’s [UNPREDICTABLE definition](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/_scratchpad/mips64_volII.txt:44) permits arbitrary exceptions, while forbidding processor halt/hang and inaccessible-state access. The instruction pages separately identify pre-Release-2 availability as a Reserved Instruction case. Their “Exceptions: Reserved Instruction” line does not turn the field restriction into a mandatory RI rule.

Consequently, field validation is a sound repair design, but UNPREDICTABLE alone does not force that design: a bounded deterministic result could also be an implementation choice. I recommend FAULT as this emulator’s explicit policy for malformed extract fields.

The tree already provides the necessary mechanism. [`X(reserved)`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_mips_instr.c:131) synchronizes the instruction PC and calls `mips_cpu_exception(cpu, EXCEPTION_RI, ...)`. Neighbouring [`X(ldc1)` and `X(sdc1)`](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_mips_instr.c:3142) reject an invalid register case by calling `instr(reserved)(cpu, ic)` and returning. These extract handlers can use that same helper.

`goto bad` instead reaches the [dyntrans emulation-abort path](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_dyntrans.c:1983), which sets `cpu->running = 0`. It is not CPU exception delivery.

The smallest consistent edit is:

| Opcode | Exact placement and effect |
|---|---|
| EXT | Immediately after decoding `msbd` and `lsb`, reject `lsb + msbd + 1 > 32` through reserved dispatch and `break`. |
| DEXT | Participate in the shared normalized `> 64` check; all its currently encodable field pairs pass. |
| DEXTM | Apply the shared check after `msbd += 32`. |
| DEXTU | Apply the shared check after `lsb += 32`. |

That is **two checks total: one 32-bit bound and one shared 64-bit bound**. No shift-expression change or new helper is necessary.

Placing those checks before argument packing and NOP selection makes the chosen policy apply to malformed `$zero` destinations too. Handler-only guards would eliminate the present UB but leave those encodings silently accepted as NOPs. Selecting reserved dispatch and breaking also preserves the [translation tail’s deferred execution during readahead](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/cpus/cpu_dyntrans.c:1912).

Enumeration of the proposed guards admits 528 EXT pairs, 1,024 DEXT pairs, and 528 pairs each for DEXTM/U. **Every admitted middle and final shift count is within its operand width.**

On a model admitting SPECIAL3, they reach its default `goto bad`. There are no three additional insert-handler shifts to repair.

**The detector needs two distinct observables: UBSan diagnostics for definedness, and CPU exception state for the selected rejection policy.** A pre-fix result-register value cannot serve as a portable oracle for undefined execution.

The `$zero` rows offer an additional useful distinction: the current decoder deliberately selects NOP, so that pre-fix path does **not** execute the suspect shifts. Its missing RI provides a defined policy differential, without inventing a wrong value for UB. It does not replace the sanitizer rows.

For this repair, **“verbatim reinstatement” means restoring the original decoder block**. The original shift expressions intentionally remain; reinstating an unchanged expression is an equivalent mutant.
