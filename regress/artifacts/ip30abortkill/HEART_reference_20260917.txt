=========================== WHAT IT SETTLES ===========================

Q3 -- WHAT SHOULD A READ AT 0x10020 RETURN AFTER THE FIX?

  **set_isr and clear_isr are WRITE-SIDE registers.**  The architectural triple is
  set / clear / status: WRITE set_isr to set bits, WRITE clear_isr to clear bits, READ isr
  for status.  A READ of set_isr has no defined meaning -- it is not a status register.

    * DO NOT mirror d->isr at 0x10020.  That fabricates semantics the hardware does not have,
      and the addition licence forbids "cannot hurt".
    * DECLINE the read.  Conservative AND now carrying a TRANSFERRED WARRANT rather than a
      guess, which is exactly what the licence asks for.

  *** AND THIS UPGRADES THE writeflag TEST FROM DEFENSIVE TO CORRECT. ***  It was argued as a
  direction guard.  The reference makes it ARCHITECTURE: executing a write-side register's
  store arm on a READ is wrong on its own terms, independently of the host death it causes.

=========================== A NEW FINDING ===========================

*** heart.h documents `isr` (0x10030) as READ-ONLY, AND THE FORK IMPLEMENTS A WRITE ARM. ***
