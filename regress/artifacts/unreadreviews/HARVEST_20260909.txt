================================================================================
THE PROCESS LESSON, and it is about WHEN a stream gets read
================================================================================
Every one of these was produced by a seat that finished its job.  What failed was the step
AFTER: nine of ten had no ledger entry, and the two that mattered most were the two nobody had
opened.  `codex_answer.py' makes reading one cost about five seconds -- and the .txt files are
all ZERO BYTES, so the only signal that a stream holds anything is running the extractor.

  **A .txt of zero bytes is not evidence of a dead seat.  It is the NORMAL shape of a codex
  job here, and the answer is in the .err.**  Score from the .err, every time.
