**3. Gate pins**

Yes, several pins can accept a duplicated green line in place of a missing or red row.

| Pin | What a duplicate can conceal |
|---|---|
| SC1/SA1 count = 2 | Two green SC1 lines can substitute for absent SA1. |
| S1–S5 count = 5 | A duplicate S1 can substitute for absent S2, S4, or S5. S3 has a separate value pin. |

| E0–E6 | Likewise protected against plain duplicate substitution by its distinct-ID pin. |
| Individually named pins | They require a matching green line, but do not reject a conflicting red line alongside it. |

These are the actual [ARM gate predicates](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/regress/gate_arm.sh:1106).

Further limitations:

- Reordering lines changes none of these checks. Reordering alone cannot turn `FAIL` into `ok`.
- Descriptive labels are generally ignored. Renaming a line can satisfy an expected ID without binding that ID to the intended measurement.
- N’s `count=1.*` also matches `count=10`; it lacks a field boundary.
- The N count and distinct-ID checks use different populations: the latter accepts any green N line, regardless of its count field.
- H1’s pin requires `named=True`, but does not independently require its printed exit/no-prompt observations.
- The additional H1 diagnostic line is reconstructed by the probe from its Boolean result; it is not independent evidence.
- The gate does not pin `ROWS=36 of 36`, and its PASS/control searches are unanchored substring searches.
- This section pins only the sites launcher’s legs, not drain/witness H1/H2.

A qualification matters: with an unchanged, honest probe, an actually failing row suppresses aggregate PASS. Therefore an arbitrary duplicate does **not** automatically turn a genuine product failure into a fully green gate. The gaps matter when a row is deleted, duplicated, mislabeled, or otherwise weakened—the conditions these pins are intended to detect.
