|---|---|---|---|

The kept-claim subset in DD3 is exactly those three rows (two decline rows and the re-arm). Their per-access checks are real. `ZCPU(0,0)` on the decline rows cannot be cancelled by an assert/deassert pair, because the counters are separate. The re-arm's single CPU assert is a total; the Clear 0x400 row pins that Clear alone at `ZCPU(1,0)`, and X6 pins a decline's interrupt count on the `irq_asserted == 0` instances.

These other multi-access kept claims are outside that subset and were already closed or disclosed:

- X6, 248 rows: `XSAME` and the interrupt counters immediately after the one access, including the repeat.
- X9: the four fields are read before the five declines that follow.
- X11: `YSAME` immediately after i's first decline. The re-decline pair's claim is "both stay latched", which is the announce counter.
- P1–P5: `P_STATE_OK` after access 1; the len-4 repeat plus the byte and halfword are checked together at the end. Each width alone is an X6 row. The four-deep history is the disclosed latch-history line.
- X10's `YDECLINE_ALL` then one `YSAME`: a cancel that needs two other latches already set is the disclosed joint.

No claimed-kept channel in that set is still read only at an endpoint a toggle can cancel.

The sentence that fails the enumeration is the one past DD3. The NOT COVERED store bullet, and the X14 header, say the multi-access rows check the five fields after every access. The reads rows and the Set/Clear rows are multi-access and do not.

**(b) Class B.** The handler touches the caller's buffer in two places, both keyed by `len`: `memory_readmax64` at the write head, and `memory_writemax64` on the read tail. Both copy exactly `len` bytes (`memory.c`), in either byte order. No arm writes `data[]` itself.

`pad_kept` is on every X6 row (248), every X9 legal read, and every X10 legal read. It observes a write into bytes `[w, 8)`. At width 8 the loop is empty.

Write-back wider than `len`, unconditional, on a read:

- Declined read, widths 1 and 2: X6, 1 site × 4 states × 2 widths × 2 orders = 16. Width 4 adds the bytes above 4. Width 8 is not a guest access at +0x18c.
- Legal reads, widths 1 and 2: X9 5×2×4 = 40, X10 5×2×4 = 40.
- WB4 (`len < 4 ? 4 : len`) is therefore 16+40+40 = 96. WB8 (always 8) adds width 4: X6 24 + X9 60 + X10 60 = 144. Both match the table. X7, X8, X11, X14 and the P-rows do not call `pad_kept`; the tail is the same function, so those rows are carried.

Fetch wider than `len`: `pad_kept` does not see a read of the pad. X12/X13 still poison bytes above the width and check the resulting enable. A fetch of 4 at width 1 or 2 folds `0xa5` into the `uint32` mask: 4 narrow shapes × 4 states × 2 latch states = 32 rows. Decline writes discard `idata`, so a widened fetch there has no state effect; the same unconditional fetch is already red on those 32. A fetch of 8 at len 4 truncates to 32 bits (the Opus seat's FW03, equivalent).

**(c) COVERED.** The DEVINIT bullet's "after EACH decline" attaches to the five declines, and that check exists. The re-arm bullet's per-access keep exists. The X6/X9/X10 pad sentence matches the calls. I am not marking COVERED false.

**NOT COVERED** is false at the store boundary: "X14's multi-access rows check the five fields after every access since 3e." That is 3 of 7 rows.
