`footbridge_pending` reads `pending_timer_interrupts[nr]` and `pending_bound[nr]`, and its one comparison splits the count into `{< bound, >= bound}`. `nr` indexes both, so the floor product is `{timer 0..3} × {below, at/above}`: eight cells. Revision 4 builds all eight, on two structs, each timer re-armed to its own rate. Struct 0 is timer 0 below at 762, timer 1 below at 1525, timer 2 above at 3051, timer 3 above at 6103. Struct 1 swaps the count and keeps the rates. 8192 sits strictly above every one of those bounds (6103 is the largest), which is the representative that separates `==` from `>=`. The owed count is sampled before the re-arm, the peak is the post-tick high-water mark, and the gate pin binds the end count `((8192-1) mod B)+1` (572, 567, 2090, 2089). R5's seven cases each drive all four timers and require the lowest peak and the highest peak both to equal that case's rate, so a single timer off the rate fails the row. That is 28 value cells, all constrained.

The filed items sit outside that product, and that placement matches the floor rule. `M_neqB`, `M_zge`, and `M_upskip` are guards added on a store the fix leaves unconditioned. The callback never splits `<` into `{0}` and `{1..B-1}`, and it never splits `>=` into `{== B}` and `{> B}`. An index taken from `timer_load[nr]` or `timer_control[nr]` is not a field `footbridge_pending` reads. `PERIODIC`, `FCLK_16`, and `EXTERNAL` are control values no row drives. Those are clause (e).

2. The new gate-14 contract still names the wrong rows for a lowering. It says a lowering is carried by "R5 (every timer) and R6/R6b". The lowering this revision's R5 change exists for is `(int) bound & ~(timer_nr << 16)`. At 50 MHz that value is `0x02FAF080`, so the mask lowers timers 2 and 3 only; every R6/R6b rate is below 65536, so bits 16 and up are already clear and the mask is a no-op. The author's own table shows that: `M_mask16` is one red R5 row, `R6=ok`, `R6b=ok`, both pins green. A lowering of an R6 rate would redden R6. This sentence says R6 and R6b carry a lowering, and the measured one they were adjacent to does not touch them.
