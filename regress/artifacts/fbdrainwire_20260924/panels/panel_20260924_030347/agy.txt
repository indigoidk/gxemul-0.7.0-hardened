#### Question 1: Premise & Arithmetic Audit
* **Arithmetic and Truncation**:
  [`src/machines/machine_cats.c:56-57`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/machines/machine_cats.c#L56-L57) sets `emulated_hz = 50000000`. With `TIMER_FCLK_256` and load 256, [`src/devices/dev_footbridge.c:234-268`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_footbridge.c#L234-L268) yields `cycles = 256 * 256 = 65536`.
  `freq = 50000000.0 / 65536.0 = 762.939453125`.
  `bound = (int) 762.939453125 = 762`.
  Because [`footbridge_pending`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_footbridge.c#L124-L130) resets to `1` when `pending_timer_interrupts[nr] >= pending_bound[nr]`, the counter cycles through `1 .. 762`. Its peak value is structurally `762`. Setting `--expect-cap 762` is indeed the tightest integer bound that will not false-fail on a correct binary.
* **Can any timer be armed at a DIFFERENT rate by the probe?**:
  **Yes**.
  1. [`regress/fbpending_drain_probe.py:112-115`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/regress/fbpending_drain_probe.py#L112-L115) defines `--fast` (`LOAD_RUN = 1`, `CTRL_ON = 0x80`, unprescaled 50 MHz). Under `--fast`, `pending_bound` is calculated as 50,000,000, not 762. (The proposed gate run does not pass `--fast`, but the capability exists in the probe).
  2. In Step 3 (freezing the timer), [`regress/fbpending_drain_probe.py:245-246`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/regress/fbpending_drain_probe.py#L245-L246) writes `TIMER_1_LOAD = 0x00FFFFFF`. In [`src/devices/dev_footbridge.c:663-664`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_footbridge.c#L663-L664), writing `TIMER_1_LOAD` invokes [`reload_timer_value`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/devices/dev_footbridge.c#L271-L349), which updates `freq` to `0.0116 Hz` and sets `pending_bound[timer_nr] = 1` (via the `bound >= 1.0` floor). Crucially, `d->pending_timer_interrupts[timer_nr]` is **not** cleared by a load write. And because the timer rate drops to 1 tick per ~86 seconds, no ticks fire during the subsequent ~0.5 s drain phase, freezing the accrued backlog in place.
* **Does 4 s accrual always exceed 762 ticks on a loaded host?**:
  At 762.94 Hz, 762 ticks requires `762 / 762.939... = 0.9987` seconds of emulated time. At 4.0 seconds, the uncapped expected backlog is ~3,051 ticks (~4x safety margin).
  GXemul’s timer engine ([`src/core/timer.c:281-291`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/core/timer.c#L281-L291)) synchronizes against real wall time via `gettimeofday` and catches up in bursts of up to `TIMER_MAX_CATCHUP = 1048576` ([`src/include/timer.h:55-59`](file:///C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/src/include/timer.h#L55-L59)) ticks per `SIGALRM`.
  On the **fixed** build, `bD <= 762` holds regardless of whether the count reached 762 or remained lower (the bound is structurally `<= 762`).
  On an **unbounded mutant**, if host starvation caused the process to receive less than ~1.0 second of wall-clock time during Python’s 4.0 s sleep, the mutant backlog could remain `< 762`, causing a false pass. The 4.0 s window provides adequate headroom under typical test environments.
