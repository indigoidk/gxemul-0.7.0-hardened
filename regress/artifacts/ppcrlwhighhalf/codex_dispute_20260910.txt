The mask’s width and wrapping boundary are explicit elsewhere:

> The mask is 64 bits long and consists of `1' bits from a start bit, Mstart, through and including a stop bit, Mstop, and `0' bits elsewhere.

The same line says:

Both are at [line 6879](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/_scratchpad/ppc_pem_32and64_2005.txt:6879), §4.2.1.4.

`rlwinm`’s RTL is at [line 30064](C:/DocumentNoSnc/CC/GXEMUL/_scratchpad/ppc_pem_32and64_2005.txt:30064), printed page 538. Here it is with statement breaks and the assignment arrows missing from the extraction restored:

```text
n  ← SH
r  ← ROTL[32](rS[32-63], n)
m  ← MASK(MB + 32, ME + 32)
rA ← r & m
```

Let `R` denote the rotated 32-bit word. The rotation produces `R || R`. When `MB > ME`, the mask selects:

```text
MB+32 .. 63
0     .. ME+32
```

Since `ME+32 ≥ 32`, the second interval contains **every position 0..31**. Consequently, the RTL puts the complete `R` into the upper half. The lower half receives the masked version of `R`; it need not contain the complete word.

For example, this is a direct evaluation of the specification:

```text
rS = 0x0000000012345678, SH = 0, MB = 31, ME = 0

r  = 0x1234567812345678
m  = 0xFFFFFFFF80000001
rA = 0x1234567800000000
```

The upper half is nonzero. For `MB ≤ ME`, by contrast, the mask lies entirely within positions 32..63, and the RTL clears the upper half.

The upper-half results **implied by these RTL definitions** are:

| Instruction | `MB ≤ ME` | `MB > ME` |
