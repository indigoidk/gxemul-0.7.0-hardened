*** Md1 IS THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE FIX. ***  The design brief's OWN phrasing of
option A was `int next = (head+1) % SIZE; ... head = next;`.  That DELETES the masked
advance and makes the mask inside `next` safety-critical -- and dropping a mask is a
one-token "simplification" a maintainer makes without thinking.  Built, it aborts with
a genuine heap overflow past the allocation.

fixA3 keeps BOTH original statements untouched and adds only the guard, which makes
that mutant inexpressible as a single edit.  A brief that proposes a fix shape can
introduce a memory-safety class that the defect never had.

*** Md3 AND Md4 ARE KILLED ONLY BY THE MARKER AND FIFO-ORDER ROWS. ***  Every
occupancy-shaped and DRQ-shaped row is GREEN under both.  An occupancy oracle is
structurally blind to any edit that keeps occupancy sound while destroying data --
and drop-oldest is exactly that edit.  This is clause (f) (the anchor rule) reaching
the same conclusion one level down: the anchored channel is a marker byte at the tail
cell and the real consumer's FIFO order, not the arithmetic under test.

Md3 deserves its own note: it is the structure `dev_wdc.c`'s own CONSUMER comment
already forbids -- "THE `return` MUST STAY OUTSIDE IT" -- so a maintainer copying the
consumer's shape into the producer writes it by accident.
