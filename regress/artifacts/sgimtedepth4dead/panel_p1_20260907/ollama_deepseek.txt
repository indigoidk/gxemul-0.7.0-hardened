### Q4 — Scope

The three depth cells (16, 32, 64) are the same class of defect: the CI arm writes one byte while the transfer copies `bufdepth` bytes, leaving uninitialised stack bytes in guest memory. However, the 32-bit case is the only one with an existing, clearly intended guard that is simply wrong. Fixing it with F1 is a one-token correction of an obvious typo.

The 16-bit and 64-bit cases require new logic (e.g., extending RGBA, initialising `buf`, or rejecting unsupported depths). They are siblings, not the same one-line defect. One round per cell is defensible, provided the siblings are filed and tracked. The 64-bit case is especially questionable because field value 3 is undefined by the header; a malicious guest could set it, but it may not be reachable in normal operation.
