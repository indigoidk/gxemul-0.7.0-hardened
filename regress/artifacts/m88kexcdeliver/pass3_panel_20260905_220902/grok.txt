### D1–D3

**D1 — REFINE the citation, uphold the filing, architecture with Fable/codex/grok.**  
Table 6-3 (`:8798-8800`) says the shadows copy XIP/NIP/FIP *at exception time*; it does not itself list delay-slot values. Those come from `:2093-2104`: while the **slot** executes, XIP=slot, NIP=target, FIP=target+4. So SNIP=target, SFIP=target+4 — the DATA_ACCESS arm, not the else-arm.

kimi’s 6.4.3.1.2 quote (“SFIP … or the target of a jump or branch”, `:8898-8902`) is FIP **while the branch is in XIP** (XIP=branch, NIP=slot, FIP=target). A precise fault *in* the slot is the next stage. kimi’s own “SNIP = shadow of NIP” then requires NIP=slot+4 at fault time, which contradicts `:2093-2095`. The else-arm is a mashup: SXIP from slot-execute, SNIP/SFIP from branch-execute.

The discriminator is rte. Manual `:9032`: “The instruction pointed to by the SNIP is the first instruction to execute.” `X(rte)` (`cpu_m88k_instr.c`, `fip != nip + 4` arm) executes SNIP then SFIP. Else-arm ⇒ slot+4 (the instruction the taken `br.n` skipped) then target. DATA_ACCESS arm ⇒ target then target+4. Those are different post-rte sequences; that is the `m88kdslotshadow` row. Re-pin first.
