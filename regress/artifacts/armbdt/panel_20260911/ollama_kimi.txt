**1. For `#525`: binding verified by pass 1, the tail writes `odata` on all reads, and `odata = 0` on a declined read mutates nothing else. No wrong mechanism appears in the packet for either.

**2. Right form, right place?** Yes, and masking after translation would be *worse*: (a) REASONED — any translation granule ≥ 1024 preserves the low two address bits, so masking vaddr pre-translation and masking paddr post-translation yield the identical physical address; (b) the difference is only *fault domain*: the raw access `[a, a+3]` can spill into the next 1 KB block, while the masked access starts at `a&~3`, which is never more than 3 lower and (block size 1024 ≫ 4) never crosses into an *earlier* block — so the corrected access is a subset of the pages the old call touched, matching the fact that the architecture specifies only the aligned word is accessed [REASONED]. So finding 6 cuts *for* the fix: the old call could fault on a page the architecture says is never touched. (c) The call site passes a virtual address into `memory_rw`, which translates internally — "mask after translation" would require open-coding translation at the call site. The mask is at the only place it can be, and it is the architecturally specified place.

**3. Findings 4 and 5 — disclosure.** ARMv6 makes unaligned multiword access to Device/Strongly-Ordered **UNPREDICTABLE** [READ]; UNPREDICTABLE subsumes both old and new behavior, so no spec-conforming caller is broken, and a guest depending on byte-splice through a device register was depending on UNPREDICTABLE. The device-offset rounding therefore belongs in the commit message, not in the code. Incidental: aligning the offset *reduces* the chance a device model is handed an odd offset and memcpy-runs past its register file — the fix is the safer of the two permitted behaviors [REASONED].

**4.
