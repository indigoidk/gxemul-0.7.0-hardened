## 2. Q2 — WHICH DISPOSITION IS WRONG (the one I'd overturn first)

**F2 — ENDIANESS rowed as part of the "class closure".**

Three converging reasons:

1. **The Fable seat is explicit and on-point.** "ENDIANESS — clause (e), not L2." `idata &= ~CRIME_CONTROL_ENDIANESS` is unconditional — there is no case branch on the bit. By the #549 pin (TESTS = masks-or-compares *as a case*), ENDIANESS is not in the tested generating set. Grading it as owed "would reopen #548". (READ: Fable seat verbatim.)

2. **The author's own framing concedes it.** The contract text in E7 lists ENDIANESS in the "class closure" alongside DOG and HARD_RESET, but the "TESTED bit" qualifier is then applied only to the side-effect rows (DOG's stop, HARD_RESET's stop). ENDIANESS gets none of those side-effect rows precisely because there are none — it is not a tested bit. The contract text contradicts itself: ENDIANESS is in the class, but the class's defining property is "TESTED bit". (REASONED from E7 contract text.)

3. **The rows are licensed anyway for DOG/HARD_RESET.** The `rearm ctl msg` and `latch ctl msg` rows do useful work catching D_nodogclear, L_dogclr, H_noresetclear, L_rstclr. Removing the ENDIANESS-catching property would require a more granular count (e.g., distinguishing 0x200-text from 0x100-text). That is a row-design change, not a license issue. The author's choice to title this as "class closure" is the overreach; the rows themselves can stay if retitled "TESTED-bit residue closure, with incidental clause-(e) catch on 0x0100". (REASONED.)

The Fable seat's "cheap optional strengthening, not owed" wording is what gives the author cover. But "optional" is ambiguous between "you may row" and "you may mention". Given the Fable seat's own #548 warning in the same paragraph, the strict reading wins. **Disclose the ENDIANESS catch as clause (e), do not list it under the class closure.**
