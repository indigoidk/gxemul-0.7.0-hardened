**D1: both sides before the ruling.**

The strongest case for `sizeof` is concrete: OM already swept the corrected form with the wide-length repair over 110 media, obtained the intended reds, and recorded no false reds in that denominator. It directly checks the current array-declarator shrink, adds no sanitizer runtime dependency, and retains the ordinary compilation environment. The incorrect earlier comparison against the unconverted serializer length was identified and corrected.

The decisive objection is equally concrete: `sizeof(b)` measures the expression’s type. On LP64, a pointer to four bytes has size eight; on ILP32, a pointer to eight bytes can have size four. That does not establish available capacity. AddressSanitizer better tests the actual accesses and avoids this pointer-size coincidence. The preserved [ASan results](/C:/DocumentNoSnc/cc/GXEMUL/GXEMUL-SEC/regress/artifacts/asiceventaddr/finalpass_20261013/opus5/pins/asanleg.txt) support the measured shrink cases, while also showing that ASan does not repair F1.

Therefore retain ASan, with these conditions:

- Measure the four proposed safe variants green under the final implementation.
- Measure a pointer to a four-byte allocation red, alongside the direct shrinks and `buf + 1`.
- Verify linking **and execution** under the actual gate compiler configurations.
- Describe ASan’s scope as instrumented access checking, without claiming universal C subobject-bound enforcement.

A short array member followed by another member inside one enclosing allocation is a **REASONED, unrun limitation** worth naming: crossing the subarray boundary need not cross an ASan-protected allocation boundary. I do not classify that as a newly measured escape.
