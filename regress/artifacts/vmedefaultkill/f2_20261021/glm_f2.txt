## Q4 -- what all of us missed

1. **`fatal()` print-and-continue is a load-bearing premise nobody named.** The entire DECLINE+ANNOUNCE design (and `p1 quiet`'s channel) rests on fatal() not exiting; the green tables prove it post-hoc (second accesses occur, latches matter), but the contract should state it as a premise rather than leave it implicit.
2. **F3's reach claim is measurable with the fix itself.** One boot of the in-tree OpenBSD 2.2 guest under the fixed binary: the `(once)` announce lines ARE the reach census (expect four at attach: MASTERCTL, MASTER3, MASTER4, MASTER4MOD). That converts the comment from source-reading to measurement and independently tests Fable's never-triggered-handler claim.
3. **Post-D2, the NULL leg fails rows in both classes by design.** Pre-state its expected CONTROL and DETECTOR failures in the PRETABLE, or the double-FAIL reads as an apparatus fault -- the same misattribution class F8/F9 police.
4. **M_ONENARROW's x86-64 kill is order- and layout-dependent** (the bit-3 owner must be probed before MISC in the same session; SLAVELMOD2@0x0c is READ-from-Fable, not packet-settleable). Pin the register pair and session order in the PRETABLE, or a future reorder silently orphans the kill.
5. **Timeout-red vs channel-red.** A loaded host turning a green row red via the 30 s budget fails loudly, but unless the row's reason distinguishes timeout from assertion, a load-induced red gets investigated as a product kill. One reason-word fixes it.
6. **F2's disposition asserts binary-identity where §4 promises to measure it** -- and F17's logs prove freshness only if they pin their inputs (mutated-source hash + binary timestamp); logs without input-pinning remain corroboration. Both are record-hygiene items in the same class the packet polices elsewhere.
