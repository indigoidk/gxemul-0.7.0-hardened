### (a) Findings that are wrong or overstated

None of the 27 findings are wrong; all 27 identify real code defects, status-laundering mechanisms, vacuous tests, or false records. However, three findings carry minor scope overstatements or nuances:

1. **`hdifffootbridge:F2` (`regress/diff_footbridge.c:9`)**: The claim that the differential is the "ONLY possible" detector is false given `regress/footbridge_sites_probe.py:293`. However, the preceding sentence at `regress/diff_footbridge.c:8-9` ("nothing in this tree boots a machine that has a footbridge") remains strictly true: `footbridge_sites_probe.py` does not boot an OS, but steps individual instructions in a cold debugger session on `-E cats`. The finding is right that the exclusivity claim is stale, but overstates the probe as an OS-level equivalent.
2. **`hdiffieeestore:F2` (`regress/diff_ieee_store.c:686`)**: While row 686 ("inf +Inf+1.0 RN") cannot fail on the missing `isfinite(s)` guard because subsequent single-precision nearest rounding maps `DBL_MAX` to `0x7f800000` (+Inf), the binary as a whole is not blind to the guard removal: the adjacent row 687 ("inf +Inf+1.0 RZ") checks toward-zero rounding, under which `DBL_MAX` clamps to `0x7f7fffff` (FLT_MAX) and fails. The finding is right that row 686 specifically cannot fail on the defect it was shipped to pin, but the defect itself does not escape the differential.
3. **`hdiffm8invread:F1` (`regress/diff_m8invread.c:491`)**: While row E9a cannot fail on deleting `regs[...] = idata` at `src/devices/dev_m8820x.c:460` because `fresh()` leaves the register at zero (reading back 0 matches expected 0), the deletion does not escape the test suite: rows E7 and E8 test nonzero readbacks and detect the deletion. The finding is right that E9a is vacuous for the store deletion it claims to catch, but the suite retains coverage elsewhere.
