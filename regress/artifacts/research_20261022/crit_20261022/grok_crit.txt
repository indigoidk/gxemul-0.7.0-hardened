## Q5. T11 — let the existing 1 KB lookup run, and do not cache it

Both exits are real and R4100-only. `dpmask == 0x7ff` is possible only because the R4100 arm ORs `0x07ff` (`:2031-2032`, `:2159-2160`). Every other CPU ORs `0x1fff`. Site 1 (`:2037-2042`) runs while invalidating the old entry, before the new one is written. Site 2 (`:2165-2170`) runs after `mask`/`hi`/`lo` are stored (`:2149-2152`), and only if the new entry has V set in lo0 or lo1. A V=0 1 KB entry does not exit. Fixing only site 2 moves the death to site 1 on the next `tlbwi` that replaces the entry. Both go in one round.

PageMask 0 is the 1 KB encoding on this CPU. The PageMask write skips `#188`'s canonicalization when `rev == MIPS_R4100` (`:750`). Rejecting it with an exception invents a trap this CPU does not raise. Dropping the entry and announcing turns a legal mapping into a refill. The slow path already treats a stored mask of 0 as the minimum page: `PAGEMASK_SHIFT_R4100` is 11 (`cop0.h:88`), so the `pmask == 0` arm (`memory_mips_v2p.c:282-289`) uses pageshift 10 and `pfn_shift` 10 (`:90`). That is the live 1 KB path. `case 0x00007ff:` (`:293`) is unreachable: a nonzero mask under `PAGEMASK_MASK_R4100` (`0x7f800`) cannot OR back down to `0x7ff`. The header's "only supports 4 KB pages" is the dyntrans limit, not a missing lookup. No VR4131 manual was opened; the witness below checks the in-tree formula `PFN<<10`.

Deleting the exits alone is the bad finish. `psize == 0x1000` already skips the fast install (`:2248`). ## Q6. T4 — advertise bit 9 in the same change; decode bit 6; 28-bit LBA; `#407` is rewritten, not a ban

Word 49 stays `00 00` (`dev_wdc.c:376-377`). The comment's `0x200` is bit 9, stored in the high byte `[2*49+0]`. Once the offset path is live, that byte becomes `0x02`. The low byte stays 0. `wd_sdh` (`:2014-2017`) puts bits 7:6 in `sectorsize` and bit 5 in `d->lba`. ATA's LBA flag is bit 6. `sectorsize` is not an ATA field; it is read back and printed, and the CHS formula uses `d->head`. The compiled-out formula (`:470-474`, `:562-566`) is the right LBA28 assembly: `(head & 0xf) << 24 | cyl << 8 | sector`, sector not decremented, `cyl = cyl_hi*256 + cyl_lo`. It is a signed `int` multiply by 512. LBA28 times 512 is 37 bits, so the multiply is `(uint64_t)512 * (uint64_t)` of a `uint32_t` assembled with ORs. 28 bits. LBA48 is not this round. Both `wdc__read` and `wdc__write`. Do not port the FreeBSD patch. It keys on bit 5, and a CHS `0xA0` has bit 5 set.

Readback currently echoes the wrong split, and both classic values round-trip by accident: `0xA0` and `0xE0` come back as themselves (`:2008`). A readback-only row does not kill the bit-5 latch. The kill is the disk offset. Readback still has to echo the written byte, including obsolete bits 7 and 5, so a NetBSD `0xA0` reads back `0xA0` (bit 6 clear) and an LBA `0xE0` reads back with bit 6 set.
