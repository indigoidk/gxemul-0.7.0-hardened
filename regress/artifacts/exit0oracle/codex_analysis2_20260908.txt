For Q2, I take the established result as given: output and PTY observations are available separately, while `exit0` comes from exit status alone.

For Q3, **capturing output makes discrimination possible; merely capturing it does not perform that discrimination**. An unexpected diagnostic can be distinguished by an assertion about its presence or absence. If the expected decline diagnostic is retained, however, matching or counting it cannot distinguish the two executions.

There are also two different failures in your concern. **Announcement followed by premature `exit(0)`** is distinguished by positive evidence that execution completed the access or progressed beyond it. **Announcement followed by failure to refuse, with eventual normal exit**, requires an observable refusal effect—such as preserved register state or the expected device-condition state. A subsequent RAM value establishes continuation but can remain correct after an improperly accepted device write.

Accordingly, the **22 value-paired sites already address premature termination at their tested access**. That is narrower than proving correct refusal. The other pairings can catch meaningful faults, but their existence alone does not justify “needing nothing.” Nor are the bare checks literally incapable of failing: nonzero exits and abnormal termination can fail them. Their weakness is accepting distinct behaviors that share status zero.

For Q5, **`pcc2_width_witness.py` is a clean, consistent precedent for the termination ambiguity**: every census row has a positive continuation companion, and missing output is represented as `absent`, not the expected value. **`gdrom_toclen_probe.py` is the stronger precedent for actual refusal**: its `bad cond`, `zero cond`, `over cond`, and `latch cond` assertions observe refusal state, including the later latched refusal. Those existing idioms address the two different ambiguities without treating survival or an announcement as proof of refusal.
