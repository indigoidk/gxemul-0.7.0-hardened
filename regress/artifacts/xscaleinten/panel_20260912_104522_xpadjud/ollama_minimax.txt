## Q2 — Which accepted disposition is wrong?

The two that matter:

F10's row is also under-specified (one form, no enumeration) but that's a refinement, not a wrong disposition.

## Q3 — What did all of us miss?

1. **The premise was not applied to its own case.** F1/F2 are the cleanest possible test of "body never tests as case ⇒ DISCLOSURE," and the panel split on it without re-reading the premise. The author's self-distrust was the right instinct; the resolution is to apply the rule uniformly, not carve out cheap partitions.

2. **D_NO_MERGE arithmetic miss (predicted 26, measured 25).** The author blames their arithmetic and says "the row-level prediction was right." **That is not checkable from the packet and not a free pass.** Either the row-level prediction is right and one row is double-counted elsewhere, or one row's prediction was wrong. **Name the fact that would settle it: a per-row kill table for D_NO_MERGE, or the row whose prediction was actually wrong.** The author should not commit PPC with an unexplained off-by-one in the prediction model.

3. **D_NOCIRC mechanism is speculation.** "Likely x86 shift-count masking making the unmasked loop circular" is a hypothesis, not a measurement. **Name the owed measurement: run D_NOCIRC on a host where shift-count masking is NOT the rule (or instrument the loop to print the shift values), or pin the mutant with a row that exercises the loop in its unmasked form.** Until then, the mutant is not understood, and a fix that doesn't understand its own mutants is fragile.

4. **F10 aliasing mutant enumeration.** The row catches one form. No list exists of other aliasing forms (rotate-first-then-mask, temporary-register rotation, mask-constructed-from-shifted-bits). **Name the owed fact: enumerate aliasing mutants of rldimi's clear-AND-rotate-then-OR pattern, or disclose "aliasing coverage is one-form."**

5. **M5 behavioural gap.** The mutator text tells us M5 redirects both directions. **No row in the corpus exercises the inten-write path under M5.** This means we don't know if the write redirect actually works — only that the read redirect does. This is an unmeasured coverage gap that the panel didn't flag.
