1. **(Floor, Q1, REASONED)** The trailer's first new line, `if (cmp_fails > failures) failures = cmp_fails;`, is the sole protection against single-tally reset (`failures = 0;` placed after the covered rows). It is **outside the disclosed classes**: the brief's "edit of this block itself" refers to the @@SELFCHECK@@ block (the `chk`/`row_cmp` probe loops), not the trailer — the trailer sits in `main`'s epilogue, outside that block. No mutant in table (b) targets it. Removing or weakening this line (e.g., changing `>` to `>=` with an offset, or deleting it) keeps gate 2 green — the G line is still printed, the @@SELFCHECK@@ token is still in the source, `grep -c 'high byte'` still reads 1, and the self-mutant lane still has its untagged pristine line — while a `failures = 0;` after the covered rows hides failures on drqring (2 natural comparator failures), w57hi (4), or srstbit (11). The shapes tried in (b) cover comparator stubs, disjunct drops, comparator mutations, both-tally reset, single-tally reset on failing devices, self-check deletion, failure-forcing removal, keyed tests, and feed edits — but **not** edits to the trailer's two new lines. The zero's denominator is therefore incomplete.

2. **(Q1 denominator, REASONED)** The disclosed shapes named in the brief are tested. The undisclosed shape — a trailer edit — is not. Naming the missing shape (a `MINE_notrailerpush` mutant, or any edit that neutralises `if (cmp_fails > failures) failures = cmp_fails;`) is the single fact that would settle the floor question's "zero" claim.

3. **(Q2, REASONED)** The block comment "each of row_cmp()'s two disjuncts alone and on either side of its want" is slightly misleading. Both new probes have got **above** want (1 > 0); the "either" refers to which disjunct (got57 vs got60) is above, not to above/below. The brief itself, one paragraph earlier, says "got ABOVE its want" — the comment contradicts the brief's own plain statement. Minor wording defect.

4. **(Q2, NOT CHECKABLE)** The source comment `/* failures the two comparators counted; only they touch it */` and the brief's "the two comparators count their own failures (nothing else touches it)" both assert that only `chk()` and `row_cmp()` increment `cmp_fails`. 5. **(Q3, REASONED)** Disclosing the both-tally reset is the right call. Any finite set of in-process tallies can be reset together; closing it requires external observation (a separate process that watches `failures`/`cmp_fails`/`rows`), which is not cheap and not in scope for this repair. The general disclosure ("the self-check proves only that the comparators answer right on ITS OWN calls") covers the principle; the specific case (both tallies zeroed together) is named in MINE_reset2's GREEN row.

6. **(Q4, REASONED)** Healthy runs are unchanged outside the SELFCHECK lines. On a passing run: `failures >= cmp_fails` (since `failures` includes everything `cmp_fails` counts plus `row()` inline plus any `sc`-forced failure), so `if (cmp_fails > failures)` does not fire; `sc_g = 1` after both self-check runs pass, so `if (!sc_g && failures == 0)` does not fire. The trailer's two new lines are dead code on healthy runs. The new row_cmp probes print two extra lines under `@@SELFCHECK@@/row` (no "high byte" in the format), so `grep -c 'high byte'` stays at 1. The (a) measurement (14/14 byte-identical except SELFCHECK lines, same exit codes) confirms this.

7. **(Q1 verification, REASONED)** The new probes correctly distinguish the comparator mutations they target:
