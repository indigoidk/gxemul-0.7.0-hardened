### Additional mutants I checked — all caught

**rlwimi predicates** (`sh ?`, `mb > me`, `mb == me`, `mb == 32`):
- `sh ?` removed entirely: equivalent at SH=0 (UB `tmp>>32` of `uint32_t` yields 0 in practice on both targets), identical at SH≠0. Equivalent mutant, not a real defect.
- `mb > me` → `mb < me`: R_REV_COND in table, 13/28.
- `mb > me` → `mb != me`: caught at (0,0) and (31,31) census cells, same mechanism as candidate 1.
- `mb == me` → `mb < me`: infinite loop on wrap rows; caught by hang.
- `mb == 32` removed/replaced with `mb > 32`: `1 << (31-mb)` goes negative/UB; caught.

**rlwimi operands** (`tmp`, `ra`, `mb`, `me`, `sh`):
- `tmp ↔ ra` inside the loop OR-merge: with `ra &= ~mask` first, `ra & mask == 0`, so `ra |= (ra & mask)` no-ops the insertion. Caught.
- `mb ↔ me`: flips wrap/non-wrap semantics; caught by wrap and nowrap rows.
- `mb ↔ sh`: scrambles mask and rotate; caught.
- Signed/unsigned shift: source `0xCAFEF00D` has top bit 1; arithmetic vs logical `>>` differs; caught at SH≠0.

**rldimi rotate loop** (`n-- != 0`, `n`, `s`):
- `--n` (pre-decrement) at SH=0: n becomes -1, never equals 0; hang.
- `n-- >= 0`: over-rotates by 1 (executes once at SH=0); caught.
- `n-- > 2`: under-rotates by 2; caught at SH=8,16,32,40,48,63.
- Bit source `b = (s >> 63) & 1` → `(s >> 62) & 1`: caught at SH=8.
- `int b = (s >> 63) & 1; s = (s << 1) | b` → `s = (s << 1) | (s >> 63)`: in C, `s` is read once, both shifts apply to the same value → equivalent to correct. (Equivalent mutant, not a defect.)

**rldimi mask loop** (end test, `m`, `mb`):
- `m != (64 - sh)`: SH=0 hang (covered above).
- `m != (63 - sh)`: caught at all SH≠63.
- `m != (sh & 63)`: caught at SH∉{0,32}.
- `m` initialized to 0 or 1 instead of `mb`: caught (mask shifted; all-ones source still differs because the inserted-bit positions differ).
- `do/while` → `while`: skips body when `mb == (64-sh)&63`, i.e. when `mb + sh ≡ 0 mod 64`. Pattern rows listed (`sh40 mb40`, `sh63 mb63`, `sh48 mb32`) have `mb+sh ∈ {80, 126, 80} mod 64 = {16, 62, 16}`, none ≡ 0; but the 495-cell census reaches `mb+sh ≡ 0` cells. I cannot enumerate 495 cells from the packet — see disclosure gap below — but the cell `sh=32, mb=32` is overwhelmingly likely in any dense census. If the census is missing all `mb+sh ≡ 0 (mod 64)` cells, this mutant would survive the rldimi leg. **Flag, not refuted.**

## Q2 — Vacuous channels (named in brief)

- **rldimi census source is all-ones.** Rotate of all-ones is all-ones regardless of amount/direction/bit-source. The census is therefore provably blind to: wrong rotate amount, wrong direction, missing rotate, wrong bit-source, pre vs post decrement on `n`, `n-- > 1`, `n-- >= 0`. Pattern rows must carry the entire rotate load.
- **rldimi pattern rows span SH ∈ {0, 8, 16, 32, 40, 48, 63}.** Missing: 1–7, 9–15, 17–31, 33–39, 41–47, 49–62. A rotate defect periodic in SH with period dividing one of the covered values AND only visible at uncovered SH would escape. I could not construct one among the natural operand/predicate swaps above — every one I tried either fires at SH=8 (the most-covered intermediate) or at SH=63 (covers all parities). But this is "I couldn't build one", not "no such mutant exists"; an exotic defect (e.g., a conditional rotate guard keyed on a specific SH bit-pattern) could in principle slip through.
- **rlwimi census source halves differ (0xDEADBEEF / 0xCAFEF00D).** Census covers SH ∈ {0, 1, 8, 16, 31}. SH=1 catches every rotate defect the census is asked to catch. Pattern rows span SH ∈ {0, 8, 16}. No vacuity found in rlwimi.

## Q4 — Book I compliance

I am packet-fed; the Book I text in `_scratchpad/ppc_uisa_book1_v201.txt` is not inlined. The brief's stated architecture is:

> rlwimi: `r <- ROTL32((RS)32:63, SH); m <- MASK(MB+32, ME+32); RA <- (r & m) | ((RA) & ~m)`. So when MB > ME the mask covers the whole high word and RA's high word becomes the rotated word.

Read straight, `MASK(MB+32, ME+32)` with MB>ME gives 1s at positions `{MB+32..63} ∪ {0..ME+32}` — which leaves a gap in the high word at positions `ME+33..MB+31` (bits `ME+1..MB-1` of the high half). The fix's wrap line `(ra & 0xffffffff) | ((uint64_t)tmp << 32)` zeros that entire gap. **If the literal MASK formula governs, the fix is wrong on every wrapping cell with MB>0 and ME<MB−1** — e.g. (SH=0, MB=24, ME=8) should preserve RA high bits 32..39 but the fix zeros them. The detector's wrap rows (which the table shows the fix passing 28/28) must therefore be matching the fix's behavior, which means either (a) the Book I text at :578 specifies something stronger than the inlined MASK formula for rlwimi's high half, or (b) the detector's expected values inherit the same drift. **Not checkable from the packet; the single fact that settles it is the rlwimi 64-bit section of `_scratchpad/ppc_uisa_book1_v201.txt` (around :578).** Flagging, not asserting.

The rldimi fix is consistent with the inlined `rldimi` architecture (`MASK(mb, 63-sh)` with `& 63` end-test wrapping) and the measured 27/27 on BASE/D_ORIG/D_NULL is internally coherent. No Book I concern from the packet.
