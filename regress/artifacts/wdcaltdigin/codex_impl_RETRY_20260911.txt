**“Do not respond” means that WDC supplies no response to that address.** What the guest subsequently observes belongs to the surrounding bus and memory model. These are separate facts:

- WDC does not claim the address.
- No other device claims the address.
- The guest receives a particular value, takes an exception, or completes a discarded write.

The first does not establish the other two. A host bridge can turn device nonresponse into a CPU exception; that does not mean the ATA device answered. Conversely, returning zero data successfully does not establish nonresponse.

The meaningful replacement is **zero WDC contribution**, independent of BSY. A conditional “if WDC answers, bit 7 is clear” assertion alone becomes vacuous when it never answers; the separate nonresponse row must carry the acceptance criterion.

The observation must identify the **actual address, direction, and width** at dispatch. Absence of an ALTSTATUS log, an unchanged destination variable, or a liveness marker elsewhere in the program does not prove that this read occurred. A direct callback test also cannot prove that registration excludes the address.

For a faulting access, the positive architectural evidence is the expected exception associated with that access, together with its dispatch record. For a completing access, record its actual completion and resolution. The expected outcome must be independently established for each attachment, not learned from the candidate being tested.

A controlled neighboring responder can additionally show that WDC leaves the address available: its response must reach the requester at `A+1`. This is useful for exposing constant responders and mapped failures, although it does not replace each machine’s unassigned-address test.

Head selection can remain a coverage dimension, but invariance is no longer an acceptance condition. A constant, a masked status alias, and a skipped access all satisfy weak invariance or clear-bit checks. The execution-and-routing oracle rejects all three.
