## WHAT ALL NINE OF US MISSED

1. **Probe-family premise is unstated.**  Pass 6's "instruction-driven can never express len > 8" and pass 7's "GETENV-driven reaches every wide cell" are mirror readings of the SAME premise: that the GETENV row is in the same probe family as the parent.  The rules section does not say so.  If GETENV-driven is a fresh probe family, then pass 6's "uncatchable in the parent" is true AT the parent and pass 7's "catchable in the new probe" is true AT the new probe — and the de/debt call at the *system* level is the unaddressed question.  That is the premise the brief should have settled, not assumed.
2. **The probe caps at the chip's `len`, not the user's request.**  Section 1 states `store_buf` "issues chunks of 1024/256/64/16/4/1 bytes."  The packet does not establish that a 1024-byte *user* request results in a 1024-byte *chip cycle* on alphabook1 — chip-side splitting is firmware-defined and can deviate from user-side request.  kbc1024 passes only if the firmware emits a single 1024-cycle from a 1024-user request.  Name the fact: that the PROM's GETENV implementation passes `store_buf.size` to `DEVICE_ACCESS` unchanged.  Without it, `vw1024` (not in the variant list) and `vw64`/`vw256` are the same class but depend on firmware chain.
3. **`vt2` is under-probed by the ladder.**  vt2 refuses a wide chunk that "continues the previous access" — that is state.  The ladder has one mc2 cell that emits a single 32-byte request into one chip dispatch; the closure does not separately probe whether a SECOND mc2-style dispatch (different address, same boot string) is the one catching vt2 vs the first.  Cannot lock from the packet; flag as owed-with-the-closure.
