### Q1 — that expression is the whole **product** fix

I would write:

```c
	(*return_paddr) = (ALPHA_PTE_TO_PFN(pte3) << pageSizeShift)
	    | (vaddr & ((1ULL << pageSizeShift) - 1));
```

Leave `return 2` and the `// TODO.` alone.

`|` and `+` are **numerically the same today**. `ALPHA_PTE_TO_PFN(pte3) << 13` has bits 12:0 clear; the mask has only those bits. No overlap, no carry.

They are not the same *sentence*:

- `|` is every other walk in this tree: ARM `(d2 & ~off) | (vaddr & off)`, m88k, MIPS `~pmask | (vaddr & pmask)`, PPC, SH.
- `+` is **this** function’s bootstrap windows (`:81`, `:84`, `:88`) — base plus offset for a fixed pocket, not a PTE.

I would write `|`, and I would derive the mask from `pageSizeShift` (the same local the PFN is shifted by), not a literal `0x1fff`. `:88`’s `0x1fff` is right for a **fixed** 8 KB PROM window. Hardcoding it next to `<< pageSizeShift` is how the mask and the shift drift. `1ULL` matches this file’s `ULL` literals; a plain `1` is the same at shift 13.

The parens are load-bearing. `1 << pageSizeShift - 1` is `0x1000`, not `0x1fff`. The existing `N = 8` row **does** kill that one (`8 & 0x1000 == 0` → `VAL_A`).

It is the whole fix **for this defect**. It is not a finished translator (see Q4).

### Q3 — 6/6, and not enough. The `0x3fff` premise is wrong.

Yes, the proposed edit goes 6/6. So do these, on the committed probe (`VADDR = 0`, `N = 8`):

| lesser edit | at `vaddr = 8` | 6/6? |
|---|---|---|
| `vaddr & 0xfff` | 8 | **yes** |
| `vaddr & 0x3fff` | 8 | **yes** |
| `vaddr & 0x7fff` (copy `:81`, twelve lines from `:88`) | 8 | **yes** |
| `vaddr & 0xf` / any mask that includes bit 3 | 8 | **yes** |
| unmasked `+ vaddr` or `\| vaddr` | 8 (VPN is 0) | **yes** |
| hardcoded `\| 8` | 8 | **yes** |

**`0xfff` is the 4 KB copy.** `MRW_OFFSET_MASK` is `0xfff` on every non-Alpha build; `DYNTRANS_PAGESIZE` defaults to 4096. That is the tempting wrong token, and it leaves the **upper 4 KB of every walked page** still broken.

**`0x3fff` is not “wrong further into the page.”** Within one 8 KB page the extra bit is bit 13, and bit 13 of the offset is 0 for every byte of that page. Going to `+0x1008` still scores a pass on `0x3fff`. The brief’s pairing of `0xfff` and `0x3fff` as the same shape is false.

**Second row, required — same table, new cold session:**

- `vaddr = 0x1008` (8-aligned, bit 12 set, `ldq` disp still in range)
- Plant `VAL_C` (e.g. `0xcccc0000cccc`) at `data + 0x1008` through K0SEG, same as A and B
- Want `VAL_C`
- Extend the K0SEG control session so a plant error cannot look like a walk success

| at `0x1008` | offset used | reads | value |
|---|---|---|---|
| HEAD / no offset | 0 | `data+0` | A `0xaaaa0000aaaa` |
| `0xfff` | 8 | `data+8` | B `0xbbbb0000bbbb` |
| `0x1fff` (the fix) | `0x1008` | `data+0x1008` | C |
| `0x3fff` | `0x1008` | `data+0x1008` | C — **still green** |
| hardcoded `\| 8` | 8 | `data+8` | B |

That is the `A+40` row: one address, three values, and `0xfff` is no longer equivalent to the fix.

**Do not put this `ldq` in the same guest program as `walk off`.** After the `+8` fill, `host_load` points at the correct physical page and the fast path indexes with `addr & 8191`. A second access on that page goes **green on HEAD**. New `session()`, one cold load, same as today.

**Third row, named because you asked about `0x3fff`:** `vaddr = 0x2008` or `0x3008` (bit 13 set ⇒ `i3 = 1`). Needs `L3[1]` filled. That is what refuses `0x3fff`, `:81`’s `0x7fff`, and unmasked `+ vaddr`. I would not silently skip it: S3 invites “copy the in-function idiom,” and `:81` is the same shape at the wrong width. I would also not pretend `+0x1008` discharges it.

`0x3008` (bits 12 and 13) on an `i3 = 1` mapping is the one extra load that fails both named widths. It is heavier (a second L3 slot). `0x1008` is the cheap row the 4 KB mutant actually requires.

### Q5 — no double-count

The fill already strips before it caches:

```c
memblock = memory_paddr_to_hostaddr(mem, paddr & ~offset_mask, writeflag);
offset   = paddr & offset_mask;
cpu->update_translation_table(cpu, vaddr & ~offset_mask, memblock, …,
    paddr & ~offset_mask);
```

`update_translation_table` asserts both addresses are `DYNTRANS_PAGESIZE`-aligned (8192). After the fix, `paddr & ~0x1fff` is still the page base. `memory_paddr_to_hostaddr` on a page-aligned paddr returns the **host page**, and the fill’s `memcpy(memblock + offset)` uses the walked offset **once**.

The fast path never looks at that paddr. It does `page[addr & 8191]` with the **virtual** offset. I-fetch also masks (`physaddr &= ~(DYNTRANS_PAGESIZE - 1)`) before `update_translation_table`. Invalidate does the same.

The double-count you are worried about would be `memory_paddr_to_hostaddr(full_paddr)` **plus** `+ (paddr & offset_mask)`. That is not what the code does. The masks stay.
