These signatures distinguish the named fault models. They do not promise unique diagnosis of arbitrary combinations of faults.

**Both early-return paths deserve rows.** They preserve a reachable state that is distinguishable from the last programmed address: `C1` after transfer A. Reprogramming `P1` before either retry would erase precisely that distinction.

There is also a subtle reason for keeping `off aligned`: under deletion of the enable guard, `off cursor` still encounters the alignment guard. Its **value assertion alone remains green**. `off aligned` exposes the missing enable guard through a fresh response and changed field; `off cursor` independently pins guard ordering through the absence of the alignment diagnostic.

For `bad cursor`, the valid descriptor planted at `C1` makes removal of the alignment guard produce a response and another cursor update. That gives a direct counterfactual for its unchanged-buffer and unchanged-field assertions.
